# Photosynthesis — 60 second film

Middle school (ages 11–14). Runtime 60.00s, 1920×1080, 30fps.

The film is designed to work with sound but **without** a voice-over: the
on-screen typography carries the whole explanation, and the score is scored to
the beats. The narration below is optional — read it live over the film, or
record it — and it is timed to fit the picture with room to breathe.

## What the film deliberately simplifies

Worth saying out loud if a student asks — these are the honest edges of the
one-minute version:

- **Two stages, shown as one.** The light-dependent reactions (in the
  thylakoid membranes) and the Calvin cycle (in the stroma) are compressed
  into a single "split, then rebuild" idea. ATP and NADPH are not named.
- **The oxygen comes from the water, not the carbon dioxide.** The film shows
  water splitting and oxygen leaving right after, which is the correct
  sequence, but it is easy to mis-remember — worth pointing out.
- **The glucose model is a stylised ring.** It reads as a six-carbon ring with
  hydroxyl groups; it is not a stereochemically accurate α-D-glucopyranose.
- **The equation is the net summary.** Real photosynthesis is dozens of
  enzyme-catalysed steps, not one reaction.
- **Chloroplast count.** A single chloroplast stands in for the ~50 or so in a
  typical leaf cell.

## Follow-up questions for the class

1. If the oxygen comes from water, what happens to the oxygen atoms that
   arrived in the carbon dioxide?
2. Why are leaves usually flat and wide rather than round and thick?
3. A plant is left in a dark cupboard for a week. Which of the three
   ingredients has it run out of, and what stops happening?
4. Where does the mass of a tree trunk come from? (Almost all of it arrives as
   a gas.)
